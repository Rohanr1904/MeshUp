# NearBird — Privacy policy vs code review (Playbook C)

> Prepared 2026-10-08 by the `legal-compliance` agent. Reviewed: root `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` (draft dated 2026-10-08) against `main`-based code in branch `docs/legal-work`. Paths relative to the repository root.
> Prepared for review by a qualified lawyer; not legal advice.

## Result

The core promises hold in code: Internet features default to OFF and are enforced at the HTTP/WebSocket layer, private messages and outbox payloads are encrypted at rest, unsent messages expire after 1 hour, and backup is disabled. But **six statements fail** or are incomplete: the permission list omits microphone, camera and Wi-Fi; the log claim is stronger than the code (Decision 016 defers the clean-up); media files, retention limits and the Google location/geocoder components are not mentioned; and the claim that location is used only after opt-in is not proven. A corrected policy is in `proposals/PRIVACY_POLICY.filled.md`.

## DPDP Act 2023 / Rules 2025 (all NEEDS LAWYER)

- **Timeline (SECONDARY).** AZB & Partners (https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/, checked 2026-10-08) says the Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025, Phase II (consent managers) applies from 13 Nov 2026, and the substantive framework from 13 May 2027 ("extended transition period of 18 months"). A web search the same day found one tracker (dcomply) giving 14 May 2027 for the 18-month tranche, and a report that MeitY floated shortening the window to 12 months (not notified as of Sept 2026, single source). I could not read the Gazette notification (G.S.R. 843(E) per one source). **I did not add a DPDP date sentence to the policy**, because the exact date is not confirmed from a primary source. The policy does not need it.
- **Is the developer a Data Fiduciary?** A Data Fiduciary "determines the purpose and means of processing" personal data. NearBird has no servers and the developer receives no data. Personal data stays on users' devices or goes to third-party Nostr relays and Google components that the user's own device contacts. An argument exists that the developer is not a fiduciary. Counter-points: the developer chooses the default relays and the ML Kit/Play Services components; Google's ML Kit metrics flow to Google. NEEDS LAWYER (LEGAL_RESEARCH §5 Q3). Until answered, the policy is written as a transparency document.
- **Grievance contact.** The proposed policy includes a grievance/privacy contact line pointing to the same placeholder mailbox. Whether DPDP requires a named grievance officer for this developer depends on the fiduciary question. A public contact does no harm.
- **Children (<18).** The Act treats under-18s as children and needs verifiable parental consent for processing their data (child definition from the Act; not re-verified against the gazette text in this review: UNKNOWN). The research baseline recommends **18+** because strangers nearby can message a user and there is no moderation or reporting. The proposed policy states 18+. The owner may override this. The app has no age gate, so the statement is a policy term, not a technical control. Lawyer: is a terms-based 18+ sufficient, or is an in-app confirmation advisable?
- **Notice content.** If the Act applies, the notice must itemise personal data and purposes, give the rights and complaint route (Data Protection Board). The policy covers what is stored, shared and why; a lawyer should check it against the notice requirements.
- **Cross-border.** Nostr relays and Google can be outside India. The Act allows transfers except to countries the government restricts. UNKNOWN whether any are restricted.

## GDPR (only if EU distribution is intended)

Distribution is GitHub Releases plus a website, with no EU targeting in the product decisions (Decision 016). If EU users are targeted: the same on-device analysis applies; the household exemption covers users' own messaging but not necessarily the developer's choices of defaults. Article 3(2) (offering to EU data subjects) would need a lawyer. NEEDS LAWYER, low priority. The policy states no EU-specific rights. Recommendation: do not market in the EU until reviewed.
